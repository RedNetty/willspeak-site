About this notebook

The home page shows the headline numbers. This page shows the lab work under them: 336 training runs in 51 recipe families read from my training logs, the test results behind each chart on the home page, and the experiments that did not work.

One thing to know before reading any of it: the same test clips were scored again and again across many runs, so every comparison here is exploratory. A recipe that wins on these clips has partly been chosen on them.

  • 425test words in the main lab set: M01 169, M02 95, M04 161. Most results count wrong words out of these.
  • 5.56points of dev WER for one word on M01's dev set (4 clips, 18 words). Dev curves are noisy and are not the test result.
  • 19words: a pooled change had to be bigger than this to count, with two of three speakers moving the same way.

The model

The recogniser is NVIDIA's Parakeet-TDT 0.6B: a stack of encoder blocks that turns sound into a sequence of vectors, and a small decoder that turns those into words. After pretraining it on the other severe speakers, I freeze the model and train one added layer after an encoder block, the adapter, with 1,049,600 trainable numbers. The map below shows where each part sits and which parts my experiments touched.

The recogniser on the device, from sound in to words out. Each block and part opens the lab tests that changed, probed or exported it.

Next03 / Training

Training

Each adapter and whole-block run here trains on one speaker's clips for a fixed number of epochs and saves the average of the last 4. After each epoch it scores that speaker's small dev set. Those dev sets are tiny: M01 has 4 clips and 18 words, M04 9 clips and 25 words, M02 24 clips and 69 words, so one word moves the curve by 5.56, 4.0 and 1.45 points.

Train loss is not comparable across recipes that start from different models or write their text in a different form. Read the curves for their shape, and the test counts for the result.

What didn't work

Most ideas I tested did not beat their control. Each row links the lab note with the full table and its caveats.

  1. Blend stock and whole-block weights300 wrong of 425 against 291 (whole blocks).No blend beat the full whole-block model; nearly all the gain was M02's.lab-01-weight-interpolation
  2. Add other speakers' weight changes to stock306 wrong of 425 against 291 (own whole blocks).Averaging weights does far less than training on the other speakers.lab-02-weight-merging
  3. Open more encoder blocks (blocks 1 to 16)285 wrong of 425 against 291 (blocks 1 to 8).Beyond about eight blocks the depth is not a lever.lab-09-depth-sweep
  4. Train only the feed-forward parts312 wrong of 425 against 291 (whole blocks).Recovered little of the whole-block gain.lab-08-feed-forward-only
  5. Pretrain on severe speakers, no adapter248 wrong of 425 against 248 (F-1).Level with F-1; pretraining only paid off once the adapter went on top.lab-11-severe-pretraining
  6. Self-train on the model's own snapped answers313 wrong of 425 against 291 (true transcripts).Three rounds came nowhere near training on true transcripts.lab-12-pseudo-label-loop
  7. Average five seeds' adapters (a soup)209 wrong of 425 against 212.4 (mean single seed).No better than one seed, so the device needs no soup step.f2-adapter-soup

  10. Train on sentences only (no single words)246.0 wrong of 425 against 212 (both kinds).Both kinds of clip are needed.f2-adapter-levers
